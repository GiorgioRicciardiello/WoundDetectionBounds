\section{Introduction}

\subsection{The Scratch-Wound Assay}

The scratch-wound (or wound-healing) assay is one of the most widely used \emph{in vitro}
methods for studying collective cell migration
\citep{Liang2007,Jonkman2014,Grada2017}.
A confluent cell monolayer is mechanically disrupted ---
typically by drawing a pipette tip across the surface --- creating a cell-free gap.
Time-lapse microscopy then records the progressive closure of this gap as cells at the wound
margin proliferate and migrate inward.
The rate and completeness of wound closure serve as proxies for migratory capacity and are
routinely used to evaluate drug effects, gene knockdowns, and phenotypic differences between
cell lines \citep{Yarrow2004,Ashby2012,Pinto2023}.
The assay's simplicity, low cost, and compatibility with high-content screening platforms have
made it a workhorse of cell biology for over two decades \citep{Stamm2016}.

\subsection{Quantification Remains a Bottleneck}

Despite the assay's conceptual simplicity, quantification remains a significant bottleneck.
Manual measurement of wound area from exported images introduces inter-observer variability,
is labor-intensive --- especially for multi-well, multi-timepoint experiments --- and does not
scale to the hundreds of trajectories generated by modern high-content screening platforms.
Several computational tools have been proposed, including TScratch \citep{Gebaeck2009},
ImageJ plugins \citep{Suarez2020}, the Robust Quantitative Scratch Assay \citep{Vargas2016},
AIM \citep{Nunes2017}, CellProfiler pipelines \citep{McQuin2018},
and more recently level-set methods with spatial sector analysis \citep{Martinotti2025}.
However, they share common limitations.

\textbf{Single-frame independence.}
Most tools process each image in isolation, ignoring the temporal structure of the experiment
and producing noisy area-versus-time curves with non-biological fluctuations ---
apparent wound re-opening that contradicts the physical reality of the assay
\citep{Treloar2013}.
This is particularly problematic when computing derived metrics such as closure speed,
where non-monotonic noise propagates into erroneous rate estimates.

\textbf{Training data requirements.}
Machine-learning approaches, including U-Net-based architectures
\citep{Ronneberger2015,Dogru2024,Naser2025}
and foundation models such as the Segment Anything Model \citep{Lehmann2024},
require annotated ground-truth masks.
These annotations are themselves subject to observer bias and are expensive to produce at scale.
Deep-learning pipelines further introduce domain-shift risks when applied to imaging conditions
different from their training distribution \citep{Oldenburg2021}.

\textbf{Lack of biological constraints.}
No existing tool enforces the physical reality that a properly conducted scratch wound can only
close over time.
While post-hoc smoothing can reduce noise, it does not encode the underlying biological axiom
and may introduce artifacts of its own.

\subsection{Biological Context: Myoblast Migration Under Pharmacological Modulation}

Cell migration and proliferation are fundamental processes governing tissue repair,
development, and pathological states including fibrosis and metastatic dissemination
\citep{Haeger2013,Ele2020}.
In skeletal muscle, satellite cell activation, migration, and differentiation are critical for
regeneration following injury \citep{Goetsch2013,Webster2013,Kowalski2017}.
The scratch-wound assay provides a reproducible \emph{in vitro} model for studying these
processes in myoblasts \citep{Goetsch2011}.

Two pharmacological targets are of particular interest in the context of skeletal muscle repair.

\textbf{The renin--angiotensin system (RAS).}
Angiotensin~II (AngII), acting through the AT1 receptor (AT1R), exerts pleiotropic effects on
skeletal muscle biology, including inhibition of satellite cell proliferation
\citep{Yoshida2013}, regulation of satellite cell chemotaxis \citep{Yoshida2010},
and promotion of myofibroblast transdifferentiation \citep{Lemos2019}.
AT1R blockade with losartan was shown to attenuate TGF-$\beta$-induced failure of muscle
regeneration in multiple myopathic states \citep{Cohn2007} and to improve muscle regeneration
in non-dystrophic injury models \citep{Bedair2008}.
These findings position AT1R blockers (ARBs) as potential modulators of myoblast migration,
though the effects are context-dependent: AT1R deficiency in skin wound models delays healing
and reduces angiogenesis \citep{Kurosaka2009}.
Candesartan, a selective AT1R antagonist, was selected for this study as a clinically available
ARB with well-characterized pharmacology \citep{Cabello2015}.

\textbf{The TGF-$\beta$/ALK5 pathway.}
Transforming growth factor-$\beta$ (TGF-$\beta$) signaling through ALK5
(TGF-$\beta$ receptor type~I) plays a dual role in skeletal muscle:
it drives myogenic-to-fibrotic cell fate switching \citep{Li2004,Loiselle2009},
suppresses myoblast differentiation via Smad3-mediated repression of myogenic transcription
factors \citep{McFarland2001}, and curbs cell fusion during regeneration \citep{Girardi2021}.
Pharmacological ALK5 inhibition has been shown to attenuate muscle fibrosis in dystrophic
models \citep{Ceco2013,Cohn2007} and cancer cachexia \citep{LachTrifilieff2019},
and recent work has systematically compared TGF-$\beta$ receptor~I inhibitors for their effects
on myotube formation \citep{Weiss2025a} and myofibroblast differentiation \citep{Weiss2025b}.
Notably, TGF-$\beta$ also influences collective cell migration by modulating leader cell
positioning at the wound edge \citep{Vishwakarma2014}, suggesting that ALK5 inhibition could
directly impact wound closure dynamics.

Critically, AngII and TGF-$\beta$ signaling pathways converge at the Smad2/3 level:
AngII activates Smad3 independently of TGF-$\beta$ ligand
\citep{RodriguezVita2005,Wang2008}, and both pathways synergize to promote fibrosis
\citep{Murphy2015}.
This crosstalk provides the rationale for testing both AT1R blockade and ALK5 inhibition in
the same experimental framework.

\subsection{Our Contribution}

We introduce \textbf{Quantification}, a two-stage pipeline that addresses the limitations of
existing wound quantification tools:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]

  \item A \textbf{variance-based single-frame detector} that exploits the fundamental texture
    difference between cell monolayer (high local variance due to cell edges, organelles, and
    phase-contrast halos) and the wound channel (smooth, low variance).
    This approach is grounded in the physics of phase-contrast image formation
    \citep{Yin2012} and follows the precedent of training-free texture-based segmentation
    in label-free microscopy \citep{Ambrossio2022,Vicar2019}.
    No training data or threshold tuning by the user is required.

  \item A \textbf{stateful temporal integrator} that encodes the monotonic closure axiom
    directly into the segmentation: once a pixel column has been declared healed, it cannot
    revert.
    This eliminates non-biological noise in the area-versus-time curve and produces smooth,
    strictly non-increasing wound trajectories.
    While temporal constraints have been explored in cell tracking
    \citep{Bragantini2025,Lee2023,Li2008} and video segmentation \citep{Guan2021},
    to our knowledge this is the first application of a hard monotonic constraint to
    wound-healing segmentation.

\end{enumerate}

The pipeline is complemented by automated multi-level quality control, parallel trajectory
processing, and downstream metric extraction (closure speed, edge displacement, closing
fraction).
We validate Quantification on a pharmacological study of wound closure in immortalized
myoblasts treated with candesartan and an ALK5 inhibitor, demonstrating 98.9\% detection
accuracy against expert annotation and biologically interpretable results across 282
trajectories.
