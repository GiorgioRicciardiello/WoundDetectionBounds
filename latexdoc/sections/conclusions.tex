\section{Conclusion}

The pipeline introduced here addresses the core limitation shared by frame-independent
wound segmentation tools: by coupling texture-based single-frame detection with a
per-column Kalman-filtered monotonic constraint, it produces wound-area trajectories
that are physically valid by construction and require no post-hoc correction.
The pipeline requires no training data, processes standard phase-contrast microscopy images in
linear time, and produces smooth, strictly non-increasing wound area trajectories.
Validation on 134 phase-contrast images across three culture conditions in immortalized
myoblasts demonstrated 98.4\% detection accuracy and a mean Dice coefficient of 0.91 against
expert annotations.
The extracted trajectories were strictly non-increasing by construction and supported a
properly specified linear mixed-effects analysis (significant closure-over-time main effect,
$p < 10^{-12}$), confirming that the pipeline yields measurements suitable for rigorous
downstream statistical inference without manual measurement or post-hoc smoothing.
The three-tier quality-control system excluded the spurious ALK5i masks that would have
inverted the condition ranking; per-trajectory parallelism via \texttt{ProcessPoolExecutor}
scales processing time linearly with CPU count.
The mathematical formulation is presented in full, all parameters are documented, and the
implementation is released as open-source Python code.
We anticipate that the pipeline will reduce the time and subjectivity currently associated
with scratch-wound quantification, enabling researchers to focus on biological interpretation
rather than manual measurement.
