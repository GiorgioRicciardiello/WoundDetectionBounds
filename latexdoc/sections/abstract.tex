\begin{abstract}

Scratch-wound assays are a cornerstone of cell-migration research, yet most computational tools segment each frame independently and incorporate temporal information only in post-hoc analysis. This produces wound-area trajectories contaminated by non-monotonic fluctuations --- apparent re-openings that contradict the expected closure of the assay --- requiring ad hoc smoothing before reliable metrics can be extracted.

We developed a fully automated, annotation-free framework that embeds temporal information directly into segmentation. It combines local variance--based wound detection with an uncertainty-aware per-column Kalman filter and a hard monotonic closure constraint encoding the expectation that wound area does not increase over time under standard conditions. Enforcing temporal consistency during segmentation, rather than afterward, yields physically plausible trajectories without annotated training data or deep-learning models. The pipeline requires minimal parameter tuning and operates on standard laboratory hardware.

Evaluated on 126 baseline images from two independent experiments, two immortalized cell lines, and three conditions, the pipeline achieved 92.1\% end-to-end accuracy against expert review (116 of 126; the automated quality-control decision counted as part of the pipeline output). Restricted to the 106 wound-bearing images, boundary detection was correct in 103 (97.2\%), with the three errors being localized boundary shifts rather than detection failures, flagged under strict annotation criteria. The quality-control module reached sensitivity 1.00 and specificity 0.565 (Cohen's $\kappa = 0.68$). An ablation study showed the Kalman filter drove trajectory stabilization while the monotonic constraint guaranteed against non-biological expansion. A linear mixed-effects model confirmed a significant effect of time on wound-edge displacement ($\beta = 0.959$~$\mu$m/h, $p = 9.3 \times 10^{-6}$), with no condition or cell-line interactions.

The framework offers a practical, reproducible, open-source solution for automated wound-healing quantification, freely available to the research community without reliance on annotated datasets or specialized infrastructure.

\end{abstract}
