\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Methodological Advances}

The Quantification pipeline offers several advances over existing scratch-wound quantification
tools.

\textbf{Training-free segmentation.}
Unlike deep-learning approaches that require annotated datasets
\citep{Ronneberger2015,Dogru2024,Lehmann2024,Naser2025}
and suffer from domain shift when applied to novel imaging conditions \citep{Oldenburg2021},
Quantification exploits a fundamental physical property of phase-contrast image formation:
cells scatter light and produce high local variance, while the empty wound channel does not
\citep{Yin2012}.
This approach aligns with growing evidence that classical texture-based methods can compete
with machine learning on small-to-medium datasets in label-free microscopy
\citep{Ambrossio2022,Vicar2019}.
The 98.9\% detection accuracy achieved without any training data is competitive with or
exceeds reported performance of supervised methods on comparable assays.

\textbf{Biologically grounded temporal constraint.}
The monotonic closure axiom is not merely a regularizer --- it encodes a physical law of the
assay system.
While temporal constraints have been explored in cell tracking
\citep{Bragantini2025,Lee2023} and video segmentation \citep{Guan2021},
prior wound-healing tools process frames independently.
The per-column monotonic constraint is strictly stronger than a global area constraint:
it enforces spatial coherence at every column, preventing the non-biological area fluctuations
that \citet{Treloar2013} identified as a major source of quantification error.

\textbf{Robust edge detection.}
The combination of RANSAC-inspired outlier rejection \citep{Fischler1981} with
Savitzky--Golay smoothing \citep{Savitzky1964} provides a principled two-stage approach to
boundary regularization.
The polynomial fit captures genuine wound curvature while rejecting localized artifacts,
and the filter preserves edge shape without phase distortion.

\textbf{Computational efficiency.}
Full vectorization ensures $O(HW)$ complexity per frame.
A typical 20-frame trajectory processes in under 10~seconds on commodity hardware,
enabling real-time analysis of high-content screening experiments
\citep{Zordan2011,Yarrow2004,Schmidt2024}.

\textbf{Multi-level quality control.}
The three-tier QC system provides hierarchical confidence in segmentation quality.
The perfect sensitivity (1.00) for wound-bearing images ensures that no valid segmentation is
erroneously rejected, while the moderate specificity for no-wound images (0.56) represents a
known limitation addressable by supplementing variance-based QC with intensity-profile features.

\subsection{Biological Findings}

The wound healing dynamics observed are consistent with the known biology of the
pharmacological targets.

\textbf{Candesartan showed no significant effect on wound closure.}
Despite evidence that AngII regulates satellite cell chemotaxis \citep{Yoshida2010} and
proliferation \citep{Yoshida2013}, AT1R blockade with candesartan did not substantially alter
myoblast wound closure kinetics relative to DMSO (20.0\% vs.\ 19.7\% at 24~h).
This may reflect cell-type-specific differences in AT1R expression or the possibility that
pro-migratory and anti-migratory effects of AngII signaling are balanced in this myoblast
system.
Notably, AT1R deficiency delays wound healing in skin models \citep{Kurosaka2009},
suggesting context-dependent effects.

\textbf{ALK5 inhibition reduced wound closure.}
The ALK5i-treated group showed the lowest fractional closure (15.5\% at 24~h), consistent
with the role of TGF-$\beta$ in promoting collective cell migration through leader cell
positioning \citep{Vishwakarma2014} and epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition-related motility
pathways.
While TGF-$\beta$ is often considered anti-regenerative in skeletal muscle due to its
pro-fibrotic effects \citep{Li2004,Ceco2013,Delaney2017}, the reduced wound closure here
suggests that ALK5 signaling also contributes to the migratory component of wound repair in
myoblasts.
This finding aligns with recent work showing that TGF-$\beta$ receptor~I inhibitors have
complex, dose-dependent effects on myoblast biology \citep{Weiss2025a,Weiss2025b}.

\textbf{No condition reached 50\% closure.}
The observation that all conditions achieved less than 20\% closure in 24~hours may reflect
the inherently slower migratory phenotype of skeletal muscle myoblasts compared to epithelial
cell lines \citep{Goetsch2011,Goetsch2013}, or specific culture conditions.
Longer observation windows (48--72~h) may be necessary to fully characterize dose-response
relationships, as noted for other slowly migrating cell types \citep{Topman2021}.

\textbf{AngII--TGF-$\beta$ crosstalk.}
The differential effects of candesartan (no change) and ALK5 inhibitor (reduced closure)
are informative in light of the known convergence of these pathways at Smad2/3
\citep{RodriguezVita2005,Wang2008,Murphy2015}.
The divergent outcomes suggest that, in this myoblast system, migration-relevant TGF-$\beta$
signaling operates predominantly through the canonical ALK5--Smad pathway rather than through
AngII-mediated Smad activation.

\subsection{Limitations}

Several known limitations warrant discussion.

\textbf{Irreversible per-column constraint.}
If the detector produces a spurious inward edge (due to debris, bubble, or focus artifact),
the monotonic constraint propagates that error to subsequent frames.
This limitation has been mitigated by the Kalman-filtered constraint (default), which assigns
high observation noise to spurious observations.
The original hard constraint remains available for reproducibility.

\textbf{Fixed threshold heuristic.}
The edge-detection threshold ($\tau = 0.5 \cdot \text{median}(\sigma^2_\text{cell})$)
assumes unimodal cell variance.
Images with heterogeneous cell populations or illumination gradients may require adaptive
thresholding.

\textbf{Integer-pixel edges.}
Edges are extracted at integer-pixel resolution.
For thin wounds approaching closure, the 1-pixel quantization error represents a significant
fraction of remaining width.
Sub-pixel edge localization via parabolic interpolation would improve precision.

\textbf{No uncertainty quantification.}
The pipeline produces point estimates without confidence intervals.
Bootstrapping area estimates under small edge perturbations would provide uncertainty bounds
essential for rigorous statistical inference.

\textbf{No physical calibration.}
All measurements are in pixel units.
Incorporating pixel-to-micrometer conversion from microscope metadata would yield physically
meaningful areas and speeds.

\textbf{Limited ground-truth annotations.}
Pixel-level validation was performed on only 5 images.
A larger annotated dataset would provide tighter estimates of segmentation accuracy.

\textbf{Slow myoblast closure.}
The modest closure observed ($<$20\% in 24~h) limits the statistical power of dose-response
comparisons.
The narrow dynamic range may mask pharmacological effects that would become apparent over
longer observation windows.

\subsection{Future Directions}

Planned improvements include:
(1) adaptive per-column thresholding using local statistics or Otsu's method;
(2) sub-pixel edge extraction via parabolic fitting of the variance profile around the
threshold crossing;
(3) bootstrap-based uncertainty quantification for area measurements;
(4) integration of pixel-size metadata for calibrated physical measurements; and
(5) extension to non-horizontal wound geometries via principal-axis rotation.

From a biological perspective, mathematical models such as the Fisher--Kolmogorov
reaction-diffusion framework \citep{Jin2015,Maini2020,Simpson2013} could be applied to
the Quantification-derived wound area trajectories to disentangle the contributions of
cell migration and proliferation to wound closure, providing mechanistic insight beyond the
aggregate closure rate.
