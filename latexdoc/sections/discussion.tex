\section{Discussion}

We report a fully automated, training-free pipeline for scratch-wound healing quantification that achieves 98.4\% detection accuracy across 134 phase-contrast images without annotated training data, parameter tuning, or specialist infrastructure. Although scratch-wound assays have been performed for decades, no existing tool enforces the physical reality that a wound can only close over time: frame-independent approaches generate area trajectories with non-biological fluctuations that require post-hoc correction before statistical analysis can proceed \citep{Treloar2013}. The presented pipeline addresses this gap by coupling texture-based single-frame detection with a biologically grounded monotonic closure constraint, and is released as open-source software \citep{Ricciardiello2025code} requiring only NumPy, OpenCV, and SciPy on standard laboratory hardware.

The single-frame detector is grounded in a first-principles observation: cells scatter light and produce high local variance in phase-contrast images, while the cell-free wound channel does not \citep{Yin2012}. This texture contrast is a stable discriminator that eliminates reliance on annotated training sets and the domain-shift risk that accompanies deep-learning models applied outside their training distribution \citep{Oldenburg2021,Dogru2024}. The detection accuracy achieved without any supervision is competitive with supervised methods on comparable assays, and the two-stage boundary regularization — RANSAC-inspired outlier rejection followed by Savitzky--Golay smoothing — provides principled separation between genuine wound curvature and artifact-driven edge spikes \citep{Fischler1981,Savitzky1964}.

The monotonic closure constraint encodes a biological axiom of the assay: wound area is non-increasing over time. Enforcing this per column — strictly stronger than a global area constraint — prevents non-biological area re-openings that would otherwise propagate into erroneous closure-rate estimates \citep{Treloar2013}. The Kalman-filtered formulation refines this further: rather than clamping edges deterministically, the filter down-weights low-confidence observations arising from debris or illumination artifacts, allowing the trajectory to recover from transient detection failures while the hard safety net guarantees the biological axiom under all conditions. The extracted trajectories supported a linear mixed-effects model with a significant closure-over-time main effect ($\beta = 0.88$~px/frame, $p < 10^{-12}$; Equation~\eqref{eq:mixed-model}), consistent with the distributional and stationarity requirements for linear mixed-effects inference. Cross-sectional pairwise comparisons between conditions yielded corrected $p$-values of 1.000, consistent with the modest closure range (${\sim}20\%$ in 24~h) and small per-group sample sizes of this test bed; these results reflect the limitations of the experimental design rather than a failure of detection.

The automated quality control from the QC module achieved a sensitivity and NPV of 1.00, ensuring no valid segmentation is discarded. Exclusion of the six QC-failing trajectories removed spurious ALK5i masks that would otherwise have inverted the area-based closure ranking relative to the edge-displacement metric. QC is therefore a safeguard for quantitative conclusions when manual review is not feasible. 

The approach has limitations that should be acknowledged. The monotonic constraint carries an irreversibility cost at the per-column level: a sufficiently large spurious inward detection, from a bubble or severe focus shift, will propagate to later frames even under Kalman filtering. The edge-detection threshold assumes unimodal cell variance and may require adaptation for heterogeneous populations or images with illumination gradients. Edges are resolved at integer-pixel resolution, introducing quantization error as wounds approach closure, and all measurements are reported in pixels; incorporation of pixel-size metadata would yield physically calibrated areas and speeds. Pixel-level validation rested on five annotated images, and a larger ground-truth dataset would tighten estimates of spatial accuracy across imaging conditions and cell types.

Future development will address sub-pixel edge localization via parabolic fitting, adaptive per-column thresholding, and bootstrap-based uncertainty bounds for area measurements. The strictly monotonic, smooth trajectories produced by this pipeline are also well-conditioned inputs for fitting mechanistic models of collective migration — in particular Fisher--Kolmogorov reaction-diffusion frameworks \citep{Jin2015,Maini2020,Simpson2013} — enabling direct estimation of cell motility and proliferation rates from the same trajectory data.

On this test bed, the pipeline produced temporally consistent wound-area trajectories across 134 phase-contrast images and two cell lines, with 98.4\% detection accuracy and a mean Dice coefficient of 0.91 against expert annotations. The automated quality-control system correctly excluded spurious segmentations that would have otherwise inverted the condition ranking, and the resulting trajectories supported well-specified downstream statistical inference. The approach requires no training data, no parameter tuning, and runs in linear time per frame on standard laboratory hardware, offering a practical improvement over frame-independent tools for this class of phase-contrast scratch-wound assays.
